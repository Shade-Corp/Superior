A wide reaching area filled with semi arid terrain. While for the most part flat, the high plains are split in half by the Wellian mountain range.

The land is greatly elevated above the rest of the continent. It was formed when a tarasque(insert any powerful mythological creature) physically slammed two plates together by lifting it with plates. Think wedge.

Covered by the watchful kingdom of Ewigkeit and the numerous rogue bands, each has taken to a specific side of the mountain range.

The plains are arid and cold.

Plant life itself tends to be fairly sparse, with only grasses and low hanging shrubs.

Now keep in mind, we're designing a *fantasy* game. We can fill in this ecology with whatever we want.

The plains themselves are viciously vibrant, with a humid atmosphere. While not quite winter, the land carries a feeling of crisp cold air, along with the freely growing pale green grass.

I want the ecology of this place to be a little different.

The area receives moderate to heavy rain fall. The reason there's no trees is NOT because the temperature is too hostile or the rainfall is too small, but because the wind is too strong to sustain tall powerful trees. The trees that are present, are typically reserved as sacred spots from the Ter of Travelers. They are twisted and winding, reinforced by the power of Fae that dwell inside of them. Either that, or the elemental power of the surroundings.

The ecology is split into two sections: the expansive plains ecology, and the narrow, geothermal borrowing cavernous ecology.

Grasses:

One of the focal points of the ecology here is the Ymgnawdoli, otherwise known as "daughter death". This is an Elder beast. A breed of a now extinct fungi, Mycharcia is a semi sentient Super fungi that spans the entire plateau. It syphons off the powerful earth energy in The Heart, where the bulk of the mycelium has coalesced into a singular bunch. It seeks to protect The Heart where it prepares to create a new Mycharcia. It is singlehandedly responsible for keeping the plateau together. It is ~50,000 years old.

"Cel" Grass, the most common type.
	Cel grass is a low highly dense grass with deep roots. It looks very similar to "normal grass" minus its paler constitution. B8F2C5. The plant is composed of two sections, much like the plains themselves(That's why it's the national plant): an above ground section that absorbs light and water, composed of individual blades, and a lower interconnected root system that expands and divides. The grass actually slowly migrates towards vibrations, following the path of grazing animals. This is because the grazing animals release nutrients back into the soil in the form of manure and the relative lack of competition(From grazing). This has led to the skill of grass calling which shepherds learn to get better grazing grounds for their sheep.

"Shubburn" 
	Despite its name, shubburns are a type of tall(er) grass. Composed of long fuzzy stalks, Shubburns are a highly nutritious yet difficult to eat plant. The long roots expand outside the ground and harden to form a rock like base. Shubburn's tips aren't hard, but aren't particularly nutritious either. There's a flowering head. The fuzzy stalk is complete with irritating hairs. Many animals eat them by stripping the hairs with a claw and then eating the stalks.


Shrubs & ferns:
"Wile puffs"
	Wile puffs are a type of shrub that grow a bulbous tip above ground. From birth, it creates a small hollow bulb which covers the internal leaves. Over time, the leaves grow to cover the bulb before the bulb makes a bigger bulb over it. At full maturity and spreading season(right at fall), the bulbs grow, and release from the woody base. As the bulb flies, the leaves inside which have grown seeds are released with the decay of the bulbous layers. The woody remnants that were left behind slowly decays to reduce to nutrients. On the opposite season(spring, when the winds reverse), the new wile puffs release into the sky and return to near where they began.

"Wail Marrow"
	Wail Marrow is a shrub that extends tubes out of the ground. The hollow tubes gather air from the wind and bring it down into the oxygen deprived soil. Thus, lots of life tends to grow around the Wail Marrow. The tubes lightly "sing" as the wind blows through them. The base of the plant is covered by low, wide, weighted leaves, which provide perfect environments for small ferns.

"Tangle Fern"
	Tangle Ferns are ferns(wow really?) that grow between the roots of Wail Marrow, It absorbs the oxygen the Wail Marrow takes in but also produces nutrients that Wail Marrow cannot. The ferns themselves are curled around the roots. Animals prefer to eat Tangle Fern over Wail Marrow. However, Wail Marrow's hollering noise scares some off.

other thingys:
"Phyngol"
	Phyngol is a moss that grows from the moisture retained by the underside of rocks. It lightly carves out a more "cave" shape on the bottom.

"Sweet Beet"
	Sweet Beets are a type of beet that grows tubers. The tubers vibrate slowly at a specific frequency. This causes other plants to avoid the area with their roots, allowing the sweet beet to avoid competition. The tuber grows assexually through ridiculous growth.

Fungi:
Fungi hold a special role as the symbolism of death. Viewed as an extension of Modron, the fungi are the embodiment of Mycharcia, the living underworld(That is, a dormant state of existence, where souls return to life).

"Mycharcia Terra"
	Mycharcia Terra is the name of the most dominant fungi. Rather than being multiple fungi, Mycharcia Terra is a singular multicellular organism that dwells from the underground roots. It spans the entire plateau and is the primary decomposer. To be more specific, Terra refers to the individual offshoots on the visible surface. The entire structure is called the Ymgnawdoli and originates from deep in ground. It has partially merged with all minor plants such as the Wail Marrow and brings nutrients from the ground to the surface in exchange for the oxygen received.

"Mycharcia Forae"
	Mycharcia Forae is the name of powerful deep ground stems which reach to the surface and absorb oxygen and push water to the surface from the depths.

"Mycharcia lith"
	Mycharcia lith is actually a misnomer, Mycharcia Lith is a humble earthy mushroom that grows large patches across the floor. Born from the shade, Merilith prime thrives in the relative darkness. It is often mistaken for being from Mycharcia but it's instead its own mushroom. The mushrooms often explode into spores when the wind picks up.
