Grasses:
"Cel" Grass, the most common type.
	Cel grass is a low highly dense grass with deep roots. It looks very similar to "normal grass" minus its paler constitution. B8F2C5. The plant is composed of two sections, much like the plains themselves(That's why it's the national plant): an above ground section that absorbs light and water, composed of individual blades, and a lower interconnected root system that expands and divides. The grass actually slowly migrates towards vibrations, following the path of grazing animals. This is because the grazing animals release nutrients back into the soil in the form of manure and the relative lack of competition(From grazing). This has led to the skill of grass calling which shepherds learn to get better grazing grounds for their sheep.

"Shubburn" 
	Despite its name, shubburns are a type of tall(er) grass. Composed of long fuzzy stalks, Shubburns are a highly nutritious yet difficult to eat plant. The long roots expand outside the ground and harden to form a rock like base. Shubburn's tips aren't hard, but aren't particularily nutritious either. There's a flowering head. The fuzzy stalk is complete with irritating hairs. Many animals eat them by stripping the hairs with a claw and then eating the stalks.



Shrubs:
"Wile puffs"
	Wile puffs are a type of shrub that grow a bulbous tip above ground. From birth, it creates a small hollow bulb which covers the internal leaves. Over time, the leaves grow to cover the bulb before the bulb makes a bigger bulb over it. At full maturity and spreading season(right at fall), the bulbs grow, and release from the woody base. As the bulb flies, the leaves inside which have grown seeds are released with the decay of the bulbous layers. The woody remnants that were left behind slowly decays to reduce to nutrients. On the opposite season(spring, when the winds reverse), the new wile puffs release into the sky and return to near where they began.
